# Richard Meadway in the Cattistock land-tax assessments, 1780–1782

## Principal finding

**Richard Meadway is named in the occupier column of Cattistock, Dorset, land-tax assessments for 1780, 1781 and 1782.** There are three entries under his name in 1780, three in 1781 and two in 1782: eight occurrences across three annual assessments.

The pattern strongly suggests a continuing occupier associated with several separately assessed interests. The documents do not, by themselves, exclude two men of the same name and supply **no age, occupation, wife, parents, children or relationship to Matthew Meadway of Hamworthy**.

## Richard's assessed interests

In the 1780 sheet Richard appears as occupier against three proprietors:

| Proprietor | Occupier | Assessment |
|---|---|---:|
| Mrs Ann Rogers | Richard Meadway | 17s |
| Mr Abraham Bradford | Richard Meadway | £2 |
| Mr Traverse | Richard Meadway | 16s |

The same pattern broadly continues in 1781. In 1782, the **17-shilling position is occupied by John Stone**, while Richard remains named against the £2 and 16-shilling assessments. This is the most useful change: Richard's name does not disappear from Cattistock, but one of the interests changes occupier.

## What can be inferred

Land tax distinguishes proprietors and occupiers. Richard is therefore not being named merely because he owned property; these entries place him in possession/occupation of separately assessed land or tenements. The recurrence across annual assessments is valuable for tracing a household through parish, settlement, estate or probate sources.

It does **not** justify attaching this Richard to the direct Medway pedigree simply because the surname is unusual. Cattistock is a separate geographical cluster until a record supplies a relationship, origin or migration link.
