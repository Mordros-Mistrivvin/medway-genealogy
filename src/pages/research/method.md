# Evidence method and coverage

This is a **working research archive**, not a finished pedigree. The central rule is simple: a record can prove what it says without proving that its subject is the same person as a namesake in another record.

## Evidence grades

- **Established / proved in the record** — the underlying record directly states the fact. Identity across separate records is still tested independently.
- **Strong / leading candidate** — multiple pieces of evidence fit, but a connecting statement or sufficiently distinctive chain is still missing.
- **Open hypothesis** — plausible and worth testing, but not ready for a pedigree assertion.
- **Excluded** — evidence positively contradicts the proposed identity, rather than merely failing to find it.

Original registers, wills, court papers, leases and other contemporary records take priority. Archive catalogue descriptions, published transcriptions and indexes are useful but are labelled as derivative. User-created family trees are finding aids, not evidence.

## Corrections are part of the archive

The project keeps material corrections visible rather than silently editing history. Examples include:

- Matthew's 1849 burial age is read as **98**, not 90; whether the reported age was accurate remains open.
- The number **20** beside Matthew in the 1789 militia material is a ballot number, not his age.
- The 1798 militia exemption belongs under **poor**; “rupture” belongs to another row.
- The 1734 Pimperne marriage is **John Medway and Elizabeth Meaden, 17 May 1734**, not a 1773 event.
- The July 1734 baptism of John, son of John and Elizabeth, is at **Church Knowle**, not Coombe Keynes.
- Thomas Christopher was **keeper of the park in East Lulworth**, not a “trooper”.
- The Askerswell marriage of 27 October 1560 is **Thomas Meadwaye and Anna**, not Richard and Anna.
- The Richard whose widow Grace administered his estate in 1691 cannot be the Richard who made a will in March 1692.
- The 1781 Matthew Meadway was victim/prosecution witness in the Hill theft case and described as a **victualler**; he was not a defendant in a Grace White assault case.

## Recovery coverage

The major 9 September audit recovered **640 selected file entries**, including **636 standalone image entries**. Exact-byte deduplication produced **429 distinct image files**; 417 decoded successfully and 12 did not. Thirty-six contact sheets were overview-inspected and 50 image IDs received documented targeted close examination.

Those figures are an audit of recovered material, **not a count of independent historical records**. Crops, enhanced copies, screenshots and duplicates may represent the same underlying source.

Conversation retrieval covered the Hamworthy line, Mary Galley, the competing Johns, Bartlett probate, Christopher family, Askerswell and the multiple Richards, Southampton maritime research and the O'Neill question. A complete machine export of every historical chat was not available in the 9 September audit, so this publication consolidates the **available, recoverable evidence** rather than claiming that every past sentence has been independently audited.

## Privacy

The public site focuses on historical research. Dates, certificates and detailed identifying material for living or potentially living people are withheld unless there is a clear research reason to publish them.
