# Medway genealogy: consolidated evidence review

**Major audit: 9 September 2026 · updated with findings through 12 September 2026**

This is the central research synthesis. It does **not** present a speculative continuous pedigree. The direct line is secure back to James Medway, baptised at Poole in 1800; the identities proposed before Matthew and Mary of Hamworthy remain separate research questions until connecting evidence is found.

## Headline findings

- **Matthew's Hamworthy burial on 21 February 1849 records age 98.** An earlier assistant reading of 90 was retracted after enlargement and comparison with the OPC transcription. Whether 98 was factually accurate remains open.
- **Matthew Meadway of Hamworthy is described as a victualler in 1781 court proceedings.** He is victim/prosecution witness in the George and Elizabeth Hill theft case, not a defendant in a Grace White assault case.
- **Matthew Medway and Mary Galley married at Poole St James on 13 August 1786.** Matthew is “Sojourner Batchelor”; Mary is “of this Parish Spinster”. Neither parentage nor birthplace is supplied.
- **James Medway, son of Matthew and Mary, was baptised at Poole St James on 19 August 1800**, anchoring the proved historical direct line.
- The **West Lulworth baptism of Matthew, son of John and Elizabeth, 8 November 1758**, is genuine but is **not yet proved** to be the Hamworthy adult.
- The leading Mary Galley candidate is now **Mary, daughter of William Galley, baptised Lytchett Minster 20 April 1760**. The case is strengthened by a documented Gally network between Lytchett and Hamworthy, but Mary Medway's reported burial age of 88 remains contrary age evidence.
- The competing **Langton Matravers Mary Galley, baptised 1756, is excluded**: an original 1801 probate commission identifies Mary Cole of Herston as sister of James Galley.
- A **1702 Herston settlement catalogue description** names Matthew Medway, yeoman, his son and heir John and daughter Martha. It does not prove that Matthew was Matthias, son of Richard the curate.
- Thomas Christopher's PCC will proves that **Pascue/Pasche was his daughter and the widow of Richard Midway, clerk**, and names a son Thomas of Andover.
- The Askerswell marriage formerly used as “Richard and Anna, 1560” is actually **Thomas Meadwaye and Anna, 27 October 1560**.
- The Richard whose widow Grace administered his estate in **1691 cannot be the Richard who made a Litton Cheney will in March 1692**.

## Matthew and Mary: chronology that must be reconciled

| Date | Record | Evidential effect |
|---|---|---|
| 10 Jul 1781 and associated proceedings | Matthew Meadway of Hamworthy; recognizance calls him **victualler** | First strong local adult anchor; identity with 1786 groom still to prove |
| 13 Aug 1786 | Matthew Medway × Mary Galley, Poole St James | Marriage proved; origins unstated |
| Apr 1789 | Hamworthy militia | Matthew is a labourer; “20” is ballot number, not age |
| 7 Jan 1798 | Lytchett Minster baptism | William, son of Matthew and Mary of Hamworthy |
| 20 Nov 1798 | Hamworthy militia | Matthew: labourer, wife present, **five children**, 5 ft 8, poverty exemption |
| 19 Aug 1800 | Poole St James baptism | James, son of Matthew and Mary |
| 15 May / 27 Jul 1806 | RG 4/121 p.16 | Matthew “(Ham)”, son of Matthew and Mary, born / baptised; likely Poole Congregational context, household identity still to test |
| 1841 | Upper Ham census | Matthew and Mary both recorded 85; Matthew's occupation appears “Pauper” |
| 22 Jul 1842 | Hamworthy burial transcription | Mary Medway, age 88; original page remains a retrieval priority |
| 21 Feb 1849 | Hamworthy burial | Matthew, age 98; age reading supported, factual accuracy unresolved |

The 1841 ages and Matthew's 1849 burial age cannot all be exact. A burial age is an informant statement, not a birth record. The discrepancy must remain visible rather than being adjusted to fit a preferred baptism.

## Children associated with Matthew and Mary

Hamworthy/Lytchett/Poole material includes Sarah (baptised 1787), Martha (1789), Mary and Elizabeth (both baptised 5 June 1791), William (1798), James (1800) and the possible additional Matthew born 1806. A shared baptism day does not by itself prove twins, and the 1798 militia count of five children needs to be interpreted against births, deaths and the return's counting convention.

## The eighteenth-century Johns

The project must keep several same-name men separate: John, son of John and Mary, baptised Swanage 1710/11; a John of West Lulworth buried in 1721; John Medway who married Elizabeth Meaden at Pimperne in 1734; John, son of John and Elizabeth, baptised at Church Knowle two months later; John of West Lulworth who married Dorothy Meech of Ower in 1754; John Medway of West Lulworth, **yeoman**, who employed William Stibbs by annual hiring; and John Meadway **senior**, West Lulworth yeoman, executor and residuary beneficiary of William Bartlet in 1762/63.

The 1721 burial is a genuine chronological barrier: that deceased John cannot be the living 1763 executor. “Senior” does not by itself identify a father-son relationship.

## Herston and the Matthias question

**D-BOC/UA/Accession889/Box13/E6**, dated 18 November 1702, is described in the archive catalogue as involving **Matthew Medway of Herston, yeoman; John his son and heir; and Martha his daughter**. **D-RWR/T/78** includes a copy of the marriage settlement involving John Dolling and Martha Medway. Because these may reflect the same transaction, they are not two independent proofs.

A candidate cluster — Mathewe Medway and An Hellier marrying at Swanage in 1665, children including John, George's 1700/1 administration naming Matthew as father at index level, and Martha's Dolling marriage — fits the Herston settlement. The missing statement is the crucial one: **no recovered record yet says that this adult Matthew was Matthias, son of Richard the curate, baptised at Coombe Keynes in 1639/40**.

## Christopher family and clergy evidence

Thomas Christopher's registered will, **PROB 11/219 p.280**, explicitly calls **Pascue Midway** his daughter and the **relict of Richard Midway, clerk**. It also names Thomas Christopher of Andover, William Christopher of London, wife Joan and daughter Frances. This creates an evidenced Hampshire collateral line worth following.

Coombe Keynes material records Christopher (1632), Katherine (1634), Theophilus (1636), Matthias (1639/40) and Mary (1642) in Richard's household. Richard was buried in 1643. Theophilus was buried 28 October 1649. Thomas Christopher, buried 10 October 1651, is described as **late keeper of the park in East Lulworth**; “trooper” is a transcription error.

Oxford alumni material describes the clergyman Richard as **son of Richard, of Askerswell**, matriculated in 1626 aged 23. CCEd supplies ordination chronology and Bristol source reference **EP/A/10/1/2**. These do not identify a particular Askerswell mother or baptism.

## Askerswell, Powerstock and the later Richards

The original Askerswell entry for 27 October 1560 is **Thomas Meadwaye and Anna**. Other Askerswell records establish surname-bearing households — including Robert and Katerine with Rogerus and Joanna — but a continuous line to the clergyman is not proved.

Powerstock contains further Meadway households and baptisms, including Thomas son of Richard (1615/16), Alexander son of Thomas (1642), John (1652), and Elles/Ellis son of William (1578/79). These are important surname-network evidence, not automatic ancestors.

Late-seventeenth-century probate and court records demand multiple Richards: Richard Meadway “the elder” in Quarter Sessions 1689; Richard of Burton Bradstock dead by the 1691 administration in which Grace is relict; and a living Richard of Litton Cheney making a will 30 March 1692. Grace's 1695 administration refers to a nuncupative will annexed and John Foster as principal legatee/administrator, but the relationship is not stated.

## Separate branches and exclusions

- John Medway of **Gloucester, hosier**, belongs to a substantial separate family unless a bridge is found.
- The incomplete will of John Medway of **Stockland, Devon** must not be merged into the Dorset line without its continuation and connecting evidence.
- Benjamin Bower's 1789 will identifies a daughter **Mary Medway, widow**; she cannot simply be equated with Mary Galley while Matthew was still alive.
- A 1743 **Netherbury** marriage of John Medway and Mary Symes of Beaminster is a separate candidate household.
- A Winfrith 1709 groom surname is not securely readable as Medway and is excluded from pedigree reasoning pending better evidence.
- A newly studied **Richard Meadway at Cattistock, 1780–1782**, is established there as an occupier but currently has no relationship to the direct line.
- The John and Mary Meadway naval household represented by **ADM 73/294/54** and **ADM 73/294/34** is separate unless evidence connects it to Matthew.

## Southampton maritime and Irish work

Earlier project work carries the line forward from James through Hamworthy and Southampton. A three-page merchant-seamen set cited in an August discussion has not yet been recovered in the audit; claims derived solely from it remain marked for source recovery. A local-history name index provides search pointers for later shipwright/master-mariner references but is derivative and must be followed to its underlying records.

A supplied Irish civil birth entry identifies **Margaret Susan O'Neill**, born 4 June 1921 in the Piltown district, County Kilkenny, daughter of **John O'Neill, farmer, and Mary O'Neill formerly Hagan**. This establishes that child and her parents; it does **not** prove the later proposed relationship. A failed name search of the 1926 census is not reliable exclusion evidence because the National Archives of Ireland documents transcription omissions and place-linking issues, including material affecting Kilkenny.

## Unproved links that define the project

1. Hamworthy Matthew = West Lulworth Matthew baptised 1758.
2. John and Elizabeth associated with West Lulworth = the Pimperne couple married 1734.
3. That John = the Swanage child baptised 1710/11.
4. His father = the Coombe Keynes John baptised 1675/6 and/or the 1702 Herston son and heir.
5. Matthew of Herston = Matthias, son of Richard the curate, baptised 1639/40.
6. Richard the curate = son of a specific Askerswell household.
7. The later Irish candidate = the woman in the proposed next-generation relationship.

These are independent propositions. A new document may prove one without proving the others.
