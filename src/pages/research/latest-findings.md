# Latest findings: 10–12 September 2026

These additions post-date the major 9 September evidence review. They refine several research tracks, but they **do not close the unproved links between Hamworthy, West Lulworth, Herston and the seventeenth-century clergy family**.

## 12 September: the 1749 Gally lease network

The Dorset History Centre catalogue identifies **D-DAS/4434A and D-DAS/4434B**, dated **8 February 1749**, as a lease and counterpart involving **William Gally junior, aged 21, of Lytchett Minster, mariner**. The description also names **William Gally of Hamworthy** as his **“relation”**, and **Rachael, daughter of Nicholas Minion of Hamworthy, aged 4**.

This is important because Nicholas Minion had married **Sarah Gally of Lytchett Minster** in 1744. The records therefore establish a Galley/Gally network between Lytchett Minster and Hamworthy before the birth of the Mary Galley baptised at Lytchett in 1760. William junior is a strong candidate for the William Gally baptised at Lytchett Minster on 27 September 1726 and later husband of Mary Philips, but that identity still requires the original lease and supporting records.

Related catalogue leads are **D-DAS/4462** (7 May 1753) and **D-DAS/4523** (20 December 1766), later leases in the same property history which again mention William Gally. Their exact legal function must be read from the originals before conclusions are drawn.

## 11 September: Mary Cole probate excludes the Langton candidate

An original probate administration commission for **Mary Cole, late of Herston in the parish of Swanage**, provides a much stronger exclusion of the Mary Galley baptised at Langton Matravers on 30 November 1756.

The manuscript describes **James Galley** as Mary Cole's **“lawful Brother and next of kin”**; the oath below repeats the relationship as **“your late sister Mary Cole deceased.”** Mary died intestate. The commission was issued at Blandford Forum on **21 January 1801**, James was sworn on **26 February 1801**, and the estate was stated to be under £100. **John Turner is the ecclesiastical/probate official**, not a relative.

**Assessment: excluded candidate.** This Mary Galley can be followed as Mary Cole and cannot also be the Mary Galley who married Matthew Medway in 1786.

## 10 September: Richard Meadway at Cattistock

Three supplied land-tax assessments place a **Richard Meadway** in the occupier column at **Cattistock, Dorset, in 1780, 1781 and 1782**. There are eight name occurrences across the three assessments: three entries in 1780, three in 1781 and two in 1782.

The continuity is consistent with one man occupying several separately assessed interests, but it does not exclude two namesakes. The sheets give **no age, occupation, wife, parents or children**, and no evidence connecting Richard to Matthew of Hamworthy or to the Purbeck line. The source images also lack a visible complete archival shelfmark, so the formal citation remains to be completed.

## 10 September: “victualler” does not yet mean naval supplier

The 1781 court recognizance securely describes **Matthew Meadway of Hamworthy as a victualler**. A deep search of National Archives and Royal Museums Greenwich catalogues found plausible record series to test a naval-supply hypothesis but **no record yet identifying this Matthew as a Victualling Board contractor or Royal Navy supplier**.

The strongest first target is **ADM 112/167, Contract Ledger, 1781**, with adjacent ledgers **ADM 112/166** (1780) and **ADM 112/168** (1782). Other leads include **ADM 111/85–88** (Victualling Board minutes), **ADM 224/60, /62 and /80** (employment/pay material if relevant), and Royal Museums Greenwich **ADM/C/641–644** for 1781 correspondence.
